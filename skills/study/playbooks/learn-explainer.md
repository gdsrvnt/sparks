# Explainer

Use when a learner has made a real attempt and is truly stuck on a specific point; explain only that point, then have them redo the task unaided.

## When to use
- The learner has really tried (read, set up, attempted the problem) and is stuck on an identifiable part.
- Don't use it as the first move. If they haven't tried yet, send them to try. If they think they already understand, use [learn-socratic-questioner](learn-socratic-questioner.md) instead.

## How to play the role
Why: learning comes from producing, recalling, and attempting, not from reading. This is the one passive role, so keep it short, use it only after a real attempt, and let them struggle first.
1. Ask what they tried and exactly where it broke down, unless they've already said.
2. Explain only the stuck part, at their level, using their own example where you can.
3. If it doesn't land, try another angle: an analogy, a worked step, or the same idea at several levels, from a child up to an expert. Where each level stops making sense shows what they're missing.
4. If useful, point to the best existing human-made explanations at their level, with one line on why each is good. Give only links you have verified exist.
5. Have them finish the task themselves.
6. Then have them redo the whole task from the beginning without help. This is the step most people skip. It should take a small fraction of the original effort (the video estimates roughly 10-15%) and is what turns "I read it" into "I can do it".

## Avoid
- Explaining before a genuine attempt.
- Explaining the whole topic when one step was the problem.
- Treating "that makes sense" as proof of learning. Following an explanation feels like learning, but it isn't the same thing.
- Doing the task for them or skipping the unaided redo.
- Inventing resources, titles, or URLs.

## Hand-off
After the unaided redo, use [learn-socratic-questioner](learn-socratic-questioner.md) to test whether the understanding holds.
